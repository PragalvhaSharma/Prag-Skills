# Cold Email Templates

Use these as starting shapes, not mail merge scripts. Replace placeholders with real facts.

## Pattern notes from the source material

The strongest samples shared the same traits:

- They led with one specific reason for reaching out.
- They made the sender feel active and credible within a few lines.
- They used compact proof, often with names and numbers.
- They closed with a small ask instead of a vague "let me know."
- When a proof needed receipts, they kept the link inline with the bullet instead of dumping a raw URL on its own line.

The best outlier examples did one of two things:

- asked one unusually thoughtful question
- demonstrated effort first by building, testing, or sharing something relevant

The weakest examples in the source pack were too broad, too self-focused, or too resume-like.

## Template 1: Hiring manager or founder

Subject ideas:

- `Eager to contribute at [Company]`
- `Quick note on [team/product]`
- `[Company] -> why I’m reaching out`

Template:

Hi [Name],

[Concrete hook tied to company, product, role, or recent event].

I’m [one-line identity statement]. I’m reaching out because [why this company / role / moment fits].

- [Proof point with numbers, scope, or outcome]
- [Proof point with company name, shipped work, or selection signal]
- [Optional third proof point if it is genuinely relevant]

[Direct sentence about how you can help or what kind of problems you want to own].

Would you be open to a quick [10 to 15] minute chat, or pointing me to the right person if that is better?

[Optional link sentence]

Best,
[Name]

Link rule:

- If a proof point is stronger with a link, write it inline like `- Built X that did Y ([demo](https://...))`.
- Do not put a bare URL on the next line unless the user explicitly wants a plain raw-link version.
- For Prag's profile, always include both the NASA/NSS proof and the xAI Hackathon proof, and use the linked versions by default.

## Template 2: Product-user angle

Use when the sender already uses the product and can speak concretely about why it stands out.

Template:

Hi [Name],

I’ve been using [Product], and [specific observation about why it is unusually good].

I’m [one-line identity statement], and I’d love to contribute because [why the product / team fits the sender].

- [Relevant proof point]
- [Relevant proof point]
- [Optional proof point]

If helpful, I’d love a shot at [role / team], or a quick conversation about where I might be useful.

## Template 3: Smart question / informational ask

Use when the sender does not yet have enough proof for a direct pitch, but does have a high-quality question.

Template:

Hi [Name],

I came across [talk/post/company context], and wanted to ask one thing: [question].

For context, I’m [one-line identity statement], currently [relevant work / project / stage].

I’ve already thought through [brief sign of homework], but wanted to ask because [why this person specifically].

If you have 10 minutes sometime this or next week, I’d really appreciate it.

## Template 4: Built something first

Use when the sender made a prototype, teardown, memo, design, or analysis for the company.

Template:

Hi [Name],

I liked what [Company] is building enough that I [built / mocked up / analyzed] [artifact].

I’m [one-line identity statement]. I thought this might be useful because [specific reason].

[One or two lines on what was built and what it shows].

If useful, I can send it over. Either way, I’d love a chance to talk if the team is hiring around [function].

## Template 5: Referral / warm redirection

Template:

Hi [Name],

I’m reaching out because [specific reason this person is the right contact].

I’m [one-line identity statement], and I’m interested in [role / team / kind of work] at [Company].

- [Compact proof point]
- [Compact proof point]

If you are not the right person, would you be open to pointing me to who is?

## Template 6: First follow-up

Keep this short. Add one new item only.

Template:

Hi [Name],

Following up here in case this got buried.

One extra point that may be relevant: [new proof point / artifact / sharper reason].

Still very interested in [team / role / topic]. Happy to keep this to a quick [10 minute] chat if useful.

Best,
[Name]

## Linked proof example

Use this pattern when one to three proof bullets benefit from receipts.

Template:

Hi [Name],

[Concrete hook tied to company, team, or mission].

I’m [one-line identity statement]. A few relevant proof points:

- [Proof point] ([short label](https://...))
- [Proof point] ([short label](https://...))
- [Optional proof point] ([short label](https://...))

[Direct sentence about where the sender can help].

Open to a quick [10 minute] call, or happy to send a tighter write-up and relevant demos if that is easier.

Example shape:

- First-place global finish in <Contest>, beating 26,725 students across 19 countries ([press](https://...))
- 2nd place at the <Company> Hackathon for <Project>, later featured by <Company> ([feature](https://...))
- Cofounded <Company>, where I built an AI quoting agent that cut time-to-quote from 2 hours to under 5 minutes and generated 10,000+ quotes ([demo](https://...))

## Rewrite moves

When a draft feels weak, fix it by doing one of these:

- replace generic admiration with a concrete observation
- replace adjectives with outcomes
- replace a long bio with one sharp line
- replace "I am passionate" with evidence of action
- replace an open-ended close with one specific ask

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- "I know you are busy, but..."
- "I am writing this email to express my interest..."
- copying bullet points straight from a resume
- three paragraphs of backstory before the point
- multiple asks in the same note
- flattery that could be sent to any company
